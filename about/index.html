Hi, I'm Andy

"I spend a lot of my week at the rink. Right now I'm working on my triples, and if you've ever tried one you know how it goes: you fall, you get up, you try again, and you're never totally sure what went wrong."

"A jump is over in about half a second. On a phone video it's basically a blur, and from one angle you can't really tell what your body did in the air. Was I already turning before I left the ice? Did I land a little short? I wanted to slow it down, turn it around, and actually measure it."

"I also really like math. I finished AP Calc AB and BC in 9th grade and I'm taking Calculus III this year. At some point I realized a jump is a math problem too: once you're in the air, it's all physics."

A jump is over in half a second, and its score is decided by eye. I wanted numbers.
Andy, on why he started Arckinex

Why I started it

"In figure skating, every jump gets a Grade of Execution, the GOE, from a panel of judges. The judges really know skating, but they watch each jump once, in real time, from where they sit. The marks are subjective, people argue about them all the time, and two judges can see the same jump pretty differently."

"But a lot of what they look for can be measured. How high was the jump? How long was I in the air? How far had I turned before my blade left the ice? Did I land short of a full rotation? Those are numbers, not opinions."

GOAL 01

Get better at skating

"I want to see exactly what changes between a jump that works and one that doesn't, so practice is about facts instead of guesses. If it helps me, it can help other skaters too."

GOAL 02

Make scoring more consistent

"I'd love for skaters, coaches and judges to have measured numbers next to the marks, so scores are easier to explain and more consistent from one panel to the next."

How it works

"It takes one normal phone video. Behind that there are three things I'm into."

01 · MATH & PHYSICS

A jump is a projectile

"Once you leave the ice, gravity is the only thing pulling on you, so your center of mass flies on a parabola. That means the air time alone tells you the height."

h = ½·g·(t/2)² = g·t²/8g = 9.81 m/s², t = 0.52 sh ≈ 33 cm

"The same physics keeps the 3D body honest: if the motion breaks the parabola, something in the reconstruction is off."

02 · 3D RENDERING

Turn it to any angle

"The replay is a 3D body drawn right in your browser, so you can look from the side, the front or straight down. Lines on the ice show the take-off and landing angles, and the parts that need work light up."

03 · AI ANALYSIS

From pixels to a 3D body

"An AI model reads the body's pose from every frame of the video and builds a 3D body from it. Then we check it against physics and against reference jumps, and measure things like pre-rotation, rotation in the air and under-rotation."

The jump on the home page is mine

"That double Axel on the home page is me, filmed on a phone at my rink (people in the background are blurred). It's not a perfect jump, and that's kind of the point: the numbers say I carried less speed into it than skaters usually do, which is exactly the kind of thing I want to find."

"Next I want to add more jump types, try it on more skaters, and compare the numbers with real judges' marks."
